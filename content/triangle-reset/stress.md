# The STRESS Corner

*The first corner of the BP Triangle Reset. 10 days. Author: Joel Polley, RN.*

First, thank you. You showed up, and showing up is the part most people never do. If your quiz landed you here, it means stress is the loudest corner of your triangle right now, and that is good news, because it means we already know where to start.

## The physiology (in plain English)

Here is the chain your body is running. Stress, real or remembered, triggers your adrenal glands to release a hormone called cortisol. Your body does not know the difference between a bear in the road and a 2 AM worry about money. It pumps out cortisol either way. And cortisol does two things that drive your blood pressure up.

First, it constricts your blood vessels. It makes your arteries tighten and lose their ability to relax, so the same blood has to push through narrower pipes. Higher pressure.

Second, it tells your kidneys to hold onto sodium. Cortisol enhances the aldosterone signal, so your kidneys reabsorb salt and water instead of releasing it. More fluid in the system means more volume pushing against your vessel walls. Higher pressure again.

There is a third thing, and it is the doorway to your next corner. Cortisol also spikes your blood sugar. It pulls glucose into your bloodstream for the emergency that never comes, and that chronic sugar load triggers insulin, which tells your kidneys to hold even more sodium. That is the hand-off. Stress feeds sugar, sugar feeds sodium, and the loop keeps turning. We will close it one corner at a time.

In a healthy body, cortisol rises, the threat passes, and cortisol falls. The system resets. The problem is not that your body makes cortisol. The problem is that modern life never lets it stop. The alarm is stuck on HIGH.

## Day 2: ELIMINATE

**Phase: ELIMINATE. Today's action: cut the number one input feeding your cortisol.**

Yesterday you named the cause. Today you remove the single biggest thing feeding it. We do not add anything yet. One clear removal.

The number one cortisol trigger hiding in most people's day is the stimulant they reach for first thing in the morning: caffeine. I want to walk you through why, and then I want to ask you to set it down.

Caffeine triggers an adrenaline release, raises your heart rate, and constricts your blood vessels. It can spike blood pressure by 5 to 10 mmHg within thirty minutes of the cup. More than that, it pours fuel on a cortisol system that is already running hot. You are stacking a stimulant on top of a stress hormone that will not switch off. That is why the afternoon crash hits so hard. Your cortisol is simultaneously too high and your adrenals are depleted, and no amount of coffee fixes that. It deepens the hole.

**Do this today:**

- Cut the caffeine. Coffee, energy drinks, strong tea, soda. For the next stretch of this corner, set it down completely.
- Swap, do not just subtract. Reach for chamomile tea or hibiscus tea instead. Both actively lower blood pressure while you sip, so you are not white-knuckling a gap. You are trading up.
- If you have leaned on caffeine hard, expect a day or two of a dull headache as your body recalibrates. That is the stimulant leaving, not a sign you need it. Drink more water and walk it off.

Hear me clearly: cutting caffeine is a win, not a loss. You are handing your adrenals their first real break in years. Most people feel their sleep deepen within a few nights, and better sleep is the foundation everything else gets built on. You are not giving something up. You are giving your body permission to come back down.

## Day 3: ELIMINATE

**Phase: ELIMINATE. Today's action: strip the secondary inputs. Removal phase complete.**

Yesterday you cut the biggest cortisol input. Today we strip the rest of the easy ones, and your removal phase is done. These are the small, silent triggers that keep the alarm ringing all night long.

Your cortisol is supposed to follow a clock. It should peak in the morning to wake you and fall to its lowest point overnight so your body can do its deep repair and your blood pressure can dip the way it is designed to. Chronic stress inverts that clock. Cortisol surges in the evening, gives you that wired second wind at 9 PM, suppresses your melatonin, and then bolts you awake at 2 or 3 AM with a racing heart. We are going to protect the evening so the clock can reset.

**Pick the ones you have not handled and execute them tonight:**

- **No screens after 9 PM.** The blue light and the stimulation hold cortisol up and push melatonin down right when it should be rising. Power down.
- **No news or social media in the last hour before bed.** Your hypothalamus cannot tell the difference between a real threat and a doom-scroll. Every alarming headline fires the same cortisol cascade. Close the apps.
- **No alcohol as a wind-down.** It feels sedating, but it activates the sympathetic nervous system a few hours later and is a common cause of the middle-of-the-night wake-up.
- **Cool and dark the room.** A cooler, fully dark room supports the melatonin window and deeper sleep stages.

**Do this today:** Choose the items above that still apply to you and put them in place starting tonight. Write down which ones you committed to so you can hold yourself to them.

That is the whole removal phase. You have stopped pouring fuel on the fire. From here on we stop subtracting and start adding the things that actively bring cortisol down. Well done. The hardest part is behind you.

## Day 4: ADD

**Phase: ADD (practice). Today's action: add the keystone calming practice for this corner.**

The removal phase is complete. Now we add. Today you get the single most important practice for this corner, and it is one you carry in your own body and can use anywhere, for free, for the rest of your life.

It is slow, deliberate breathing, and it is not a mood trick. It is physiology. Here is the mechanism. Your vagus nerve is the main cable of your rest-and-recover nervous system, the off-switch to the fight-or-flight overdrive that chronic cortisol keeps jammed on. Slow breathing with a long exhale activates that nerve directly. It tells your heart to slow, your vessels to relax, and your cortisol to start dropping. You are reaching in and flipping the switch your stress response refuses to flip on its own.

**Do this today, and make it your keystone:**

- **Box breathing.** Inhale through your nose for a count of 4, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4. Repeat for five minutes. Do one round mid-afternoon, when cortisol tends to spike, and one round before bed.
- **The bedtime off-switch.** As your head hits the pillow, take five slow breaths with a longer exhale than inhale: in for 4, hold for 4, out for 6. The long exhale is what pulls the vagus brake. This is your nightly signal to your body that the day is over and it is safe to power down.

Two more keystone-grade practices to weave in, because cortisol comes down through more than one door:

- **Get outside and walk in the morning sun.** A 20-minute unhurried walk in daylight does three things at once: it lowers cortisol, it sets your body clock so the cortisol curve falls correctly at night, and the sunlight itself supports the whole rhythm. Unhurried is the point. This is not exercise to conquer. It is rest in motion.
- **Pray or sit with Scripture.** Worry is a physiological cortisol generator. Every anxious thought fires the stress axis. Casting that worry, naming it and handing it over, engages the part of your brain that strengthens the cortisol brake. "Cast all your anxiety on Him, because He cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7). Faith is not only spiritual here. It is physiological.

Anchor the breathing as the practice you do every single day. The rest stacks on top.

## Day 5: ADD

**Phase: ADD (foods). Today's action: add the healing foods that bring cortisol down and open your vessels.**

You have the practice. Today you add the food, because what you put on your plate either calms this system or inflames it. We are going plant-forward and whole-food, the way your body was designed to be fed.

The goal of today is twofold. Feed the foods that lower cortisol and reduce inflammation, and feed the foods that restore nitric oxide, your body's master signal for relaxing arteries. Cortisol suppresses nitric oxide hard, which is a big part of why your vessels stay tight. We put it back through the plate.

**Do this today:**

- **Build meals from whole, plant-based ingredients.** Vegetables, fruit, beans, lentils, whole grains, nuts, and seeds. This is anti-inflammatory eating, and inflammation is one of the pathways cortisol uses to damage your vessel walls. Cook from real ingredients rather than packaged food.
- **Add raw garlic.** Crush two cloves, let them sit ten minutes so the active compound forms, then take them with your largest meal. Garlic supports nitric oxide and works on several blood-pressure pathways at once.
- **Lean on nitric-oxide foods.** Beets and beet juice, leafy greens, and garlic all stimulate the nitric oxide your cortisol has been suppressing. This is how you give your arteries back the ability to relax.
- **Magnesium-rich foods.** Leafy greens, pumpkin seeds, beans, and nuts. Barbara O'Neill calls magnesium "the relaxation mineral," and she is right. It is involved in over 300 functions in the body, and chronic stress burns through it fast. Here is the mechanism Walt Cross teaches, and it is worth sitting with: when you are stressed, the body dumps magnesium to protect the cells from adrenaline. So the more stress you carry, the more of your relaxation mineral you lose, right when you need it most. Magnesium is the one most depleted by chronic stress and the one most people with high blood pressure are short on. We will add it as a supplement on Day 6, and the food groundwork starts today.

One quiet but powerful note on protein. Industrially raised animals live in chronic stress, and the cortisol and adrenaline in their bloodstream at slaughter end up in the meat. When you eat the biochemistry of a stressed animal, you take on the same hormones that raise your own pressure. Leaning into a plant-based plate removes that hidden load entirely. Your plate becomes part of the medicine.

Keep yesterday's breathing and walking going. Today the kitchen joins the team.
