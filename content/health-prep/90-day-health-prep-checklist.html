The 90-Day Health Prep Checklist

Prepare your body, not just your pantry.

Joel Polley, RN

The Pentagon wants its major U.S. bases able to run for at least 90 days on their own, and medical supplies are on its list. Food and water matter. But every supply you store depends on one thing: your health. Work through this list one step at a time, starting this week.

1Know your numbers Days 1 to 14

  • Get an upper-arm blood pressure cuff that has been validated for accuracy. The U.S. Validated Device Listing is at validatebp.org.
  • Measure the right way: sit quietly for 5 minutes, back supported, feet flat, arm resting at heart level. Take 2 readings, 1 minute apart.
  • Measure morning and evening for 7 days and write every reading in the log on page 2.
  • Ask your doctor what your goal numbers are. Bring your 7-day log to the visit.
  • If you have diabetes or prediabetes, ask your doctor for your A1C (the 3-month blood sugar test) and your blood sugar goals.

Know your danger line. If a reading is 180/120 or higher, rest 5 minutes and check again. If it is still that high and you have chest pain, trouble breathing, back pain, numbness or weakness, vision change, or trouble speaking, call 911. If you have none of those, call your doctor right away.

2Medicines and medical supplies Days 1 to 14

  • Write your medicine list: every medicine, the dose, what it is for, your allergies, and your doctor's and pharmacy's phone numbers. Keep a paper copy with your supplies.
  • Keep your prescriptions filled and not expired, and ask your doctor or pharmacist about keeping an extra supply on hand. Ready.gov lists prescription medicines in a basic emergency kit.
  • Store medicines the way the label says. Heat and moisture can damage them.
  • Spare batteries for your cuff and meters, and test strips if you use a glucose meter.
  • A basic first-aid kit, plus glasses and anything else you use every day.

Never stop or change a medicine on your own. If something feels wrong, call your doctor or pharmacist.

3Water Days 15 to 45

  • Store water: at least 1 gallon per person per day, for several days. Ready.gov.
  • Water is your first drink. Plain water, or water with lemon or cucumber, and caffeine-free herbal teas. If you take medicine, ask your pharmacist before you try a new herbal tea.

4Sodium: the salt trap

  • Read the label. 5% Daily Value of sodium or less per serving is low; 20% or more is high. FDA.
  • Stock dried beans, lentils, and split peas.
  • Stock oats, brown rice, and quinoa.
  • Choose "no salt added" or "low sodium" canned beans, tomatoes, and vegetables.
  • Season with herbs, garlic and onion powder, and salt-free herb blends. Skip potassium salt substitutes unless your doctor says yes.

5Sugar: pick lower-sugar foods

  • Whole grains and beans instead of crackers, white rice, and boxed meals.
  • Nuts, seeds, and nut or seed butters.
  • Fruit canned in water or its own juice; dried fruit in small portions.
  • Unsweetened plant milk (soy or almond) in shelf-stable cartons.
  • Read labels for added sugar and pick the lowest.

6Stress: train your calm now Days 46 to 90

  • Move most days. Aim for 150 minutes a week of brisk walking or similar activity. CDC.
  • Sleep 7 or more hours a night. CDC, for adults.
  • Slow breathing: breathe in for 4 counts, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Repeat for 2 minutes.
  • Pray and read Scripture. "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself..." Proverbs 22:3
  • Check on one neighbor or family member each week. Preparing together is stronger.
  • Repeat your 7-day log in the last 2 weeks and bring it to your doctor.

Days 15 to 45Water stored. Pantry swaps for sodium and sugar, a few items each week.
