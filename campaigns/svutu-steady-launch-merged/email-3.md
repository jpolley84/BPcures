PREVIEW TEXT: Long before labs and pills, women had somewhere to turn. Here's where.

Friend.

Two days ago I gave you your breath.

Today I want to give you something older.

Think about this.

Women have been living through the change for as long as there have been women.

The racing heart. The rising pressure. The bracing.

This is not new.

What's new is that we are the first generation handed only a cuff, a shrug, and a salt warning.

Every generation before us had somewhere else to turn first.

The garden.

I mean that plainly.

Before there was a pharmacy on the corner, the garden *was* the pharmacy.

The first one. Since the very beginning — since "let the earth bring forth."

And the women knew it.

The grandmother who pressed a certain leaf into your tea when your heart was racing.

The great-aunt who "just happened" to grow the same handful of plants every wise woman grew.

That wasn't folklore.

That was inherited knowledge — passed hand to hand, mother to daughter, for the exact season you're in right now.

Here's what breaks my heart a little:

that knowledge got interrupted.

Somewhere along the way, we stopped passing it down.

And a whole generation of women — you, me — got left holding a salt warning and nothing else.

So this week, I want to give some of it back.

Because it turns out the plants those women reached for weren't random.

Modern researchers keep going back and studying the very same ones —

and finding out grandma was onto something.

Over the next few days, I'm going to introduce you to them.

The specific plants.

A couple at a time.

What each one is, what it's been reached for, and why it matters for exactly what your body is doing right now.

By the end of this week, you'll hold the whole picture —

the same picture the wise women held, with the research to back it up.

And it lines up, almost plant for plant, with all three corners of the Triangle Joel introduced you to two days ago. Grandma wasn't picking randomly. She was working the whole Triangle before anyone had a name for it.

This is our inheritance, friend.

Let's take it back.

**And forward this to your mom, especially.**

She may remember the grandmother who did this.

She may be the one holding the salt warning right now.

This is hers too.

I'm cheering for you.

Annie
SVUTU · Rooted in truth

p.s. — Root remedies for root causes. That's not a slogan. It's the oldest medicine there is, and it's your birthright.
