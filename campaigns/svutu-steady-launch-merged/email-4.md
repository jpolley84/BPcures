Okay. Let's take back some of that inheritance.

I'm starting with the quiet ones.

Not the famous plants.

The ones that never make the headline — and absolutely should.

**Nettle.**

Sounds like a weed. Practically is.

But it's one of the most mineral-dense green things that grows —

and for generations women reached for it to help the body let go of what it's holding.

You know the friend who carries everyone's bags and never sets hers down?

That's your body in this season.

Nettle helps her set the bags down.

**Lavender.**

You think candle. Nice-sleep smell.

But here's the nurse in me talking:

remember how we said stress travels *down* — into the pressure you carry?

Lavender has been used for centuries to help settle exactly that.

A whisper of it, at the end of a long day, right as your shoulders drop.

Not "just a smell." A root cause, getting a little attention.

**Chamomile.**

I know — it's in every sleepy tea at the store.

But chamomile was never just about sleep.

It's a *nervous-system* plant —

traditionally reached for to bring the body down out of that low, all-day hum.

And remember: a calm system and a steady heart were never two projects.

They're the same one.

**Lemongrass.**

The worker. Bright, citrusy.

Long used to support circulation — helping things move instead of sit stuck.

Like opening the windows in a stuffy room.

Now look what you're holding.

Four plants.

Nettle — helps the body let go.

Lavender — helps stress stop traveling down.

Chamomile — settles the whole system.

Lemongrass — keeps things moving.

Every one of them working a root cause we spent this week naming — the bracing, the tension, the change.

Every one of them the exact kind of thing grandma grew.

Here's how Joel would draw it on his whiteboard (he's the drawing-on-whiteboards type):

Lavender and chamomile — Stress Pressure corner. The switch that's stuck on.

Nettle and lemongrass — the Sodium corner. The minerals and the movement your blood vessels are asking for.

Two corners. Four plants. Already working, quietly, before you even knew their names.

Four plants.

And I haven't even shown you the two that matter most.

The two that show up in study after study for the heart.

The two I'd build an entire cup around, if I were going to build one.

You get them in two days.

And when you do — it's all going to come together.

**Forward this to your person.**

We're reclaiming this together.

Cheering for you.

Annie
SVUTU · Rooted in truth
